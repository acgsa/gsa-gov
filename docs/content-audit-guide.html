How each page is scored (100 points)

Every live page is fetched, its readable content and deterministic signals extracted, then scored by USAi against a strict 100-point rubric. Scores are intentionally conservative — most legacy pages score low.

Criterion Max What it measures
Alignment 30 Fit with a GSA line of business (Real Estate, Acquisition, Technology) or general public / federal-employee information.
Content quality 20 Depth, accuracy signals, structure, and currency of the content.
Actionable 20 Does the page let a user DO something — forms, tools, clear next steps, downloads, contacts?
SEO value 15 Title / description quality, heading structure, and likely search value.
Redundancy 10 10 = unique; scored lower as the near-duplicate cluster size grows or the page is boilerplate.
User value (DAP) 5 Supported by real traffic (DAP visits / pageviews) when available.
Total 100

Recommendations

Recommendation Meaning
Keep High value, unique, aligned, and actionable. Migrate as-is into the new templates.
Consolidate Overlapping or thin, but has salvageable value. The report names a merge target; the page still flows into the new site (nothing is lost).
Archive Historical record (press releases / newsroom) preserved in the searchable Press Releases database, but NOT migrated as live Keep content. Set deterministically by the newsroom preservation gate — never auto-deleted. Separated from Keep so the Keep count reflects only genuinely retained, actively-maintained pages.
Delete Low value, redundant, or obsolete with little/no traffic. Candidate for retirement — subject to the hard gates below.
Needs review The model could not score confidently, or a deterministic signal (thin content, JS-rendered, fetch error) requires a human decision. Never auto-deleted.

Flags & badges

The key difference: Legal hold is a deterministic, URL/keyword-matched gate that forces preservation; Possible legal is the model's best-guess flag for pages that only look mandated, surfaced for a human to confirm.

Flag Meaning
LEGAL HOLD Deterministic hard gate. The URL/keywords match the owner-editable legal-hold list of statutorily-required pages (No FEAR Act, FOIA, privacy, Section 508, OIG, vulnerability disclosure, records/paperwork notices). Recommendation is FORCED to Keep and Delete/Consolidate is blocked — regardless of the model's score.
POSSIBLE LEGAL Model-raised soft flag. The page READS like a mandated disclosure but did not match the deterministic list. Surfaced for human confirmation so unknown statutory pages are never silently deleted — it does not by itself change the recommendation.
REVIEW The page carries a manual-review reason (e.g. thin-content, js-rendered, fetch-error, redirect, 410-gone) even though it received a Keep/Consolidate/Delete recommendation.

The OVERRIDDEN badge

Overridden means a human reviewer manually changed that page's recommendation away from what the audit produced. Using a row's Action dropdown to pick a value different from the model's original recommendation reveals the OVERRIDDEN badge next to the pill — it is a visual audit trail marking rows whose displayed recommendation no longer matches the model's raw output.

Important: overrides live only in this browser view. They do not write back into the JSON/CSV artifacts (use Download overrides (JSON) to export them) and they do not change the deterministic legal-hold gate — a LEGAL HOLD page is still forced to Keep regardless of any override set here.

Deterministic hard gates

Two gates run after the model and override it — they can only make the outcome more conservative (never delete more):

  1. Legal-hold gate. Statutorily-required pages are forced to Keep; Delete/Consolidate is blocked. The list is owner-editable in scripts/content-audit/legal-hold.ts.
  2. Newsroom / press-release preservation gate. News releases, press releases, congressional testimony, administrator speeches, and the regional news archives are historical records destined for the new searchable Press Releases database. These URLs are reclassified to Archive — preserved as historical records but not counted as live Keep content. (Pages that also match the legal-hold gate stay Keep.)

Traffic data (DAP)

The DAP visits column is sourced from a GA4 "All Pages" export (All_Pages.csv), matched to each URL by an exact host + path lookup (never a fuzzy title match). visits ← GA4 Active users, pageviews ← GA4 Views. A page with no exact match shows an em dash and simply contributes no traffic signal to its score.
